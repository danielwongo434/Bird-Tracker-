Weather Radar View for Bird Migration Nights

This page embeds a live weather radar map. At night, especially during spring and fall, some of the echoes on radar are not rain but huge flocks of migrating birds. Tools like BirdCast use this kind of data (plus modeling) to estimate migration intensity.

Use this radar as a rough guide to where the atmosphere is active. On clear, calm nights with widespread radar reflectivity and little rain, a lot of that “clutter” can be birds passing overhead.

How to read this for birds

On nights with strong migration, you’ll often see broad, fuzzy-looking radar signals blooming after sunset and fading toward dawn. These can be birds taking off, flying at altitude, and then dropping out of the air before sunrise.

This project is about connecting that kind of real-world data with species profiles and educational tools. Future versions may overlay migration forecasts, wind fields, and species-specific timing.
